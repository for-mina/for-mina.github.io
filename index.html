Visa check, then the ferry

Naminara checks your visa at its “immigration office”, which is the ticket office. Then five minutes on the ferry.

Ferries run every 10 to 20 minutes.

Photo: Minseong Kim, CC BY-SA 4.0

The zip-wire tower

An 80-metre tower and about a kilometre of cable over the river, landing right on the island. It opens at 09:00.

Totally optional. Bravery not required.

Pine tree lane

The first lane after the ferry: tall pines and soft light. Probably the quietest walk of the day.

Our first stop.

The officer’s house

Home of the island’s famously grumpy ostrich, the officer who stamped your visa. Please be polite.

No snacks. He’s on duty.

Photo: Drcbrothephotographer, CC0

Bike center

Single bikes or a two-seater. Two singles cost the same as one two-seater, so it’s purely a question of teamwork.

Walking is always allowed.

Lunch at Bobplex

Six restaurants under one roof, including halal Asian food at Dongmun. There’s a prayer room in the same building.

An early lunch beats the queue.

Songpa ginkgo lane

Every autumn, Seoul’s Songpa district sends its fallen ginkgo leaves here. If we’re lucky, it’s a yellow carpet.

Lucky is not guaranteed.

Metasequoia lane

The island’s most famous lane: two rows of giant trees, rusty orange in November. Sonata Cafe by the lane makes hotteok.

Hotteok: a hot sweet pancake. Mandatory.

Reeds and rowing boats

A quiet path through tall reeds on the east shore. The rowing boats run until 15 November, so we just make it.

Boats until 17:00.

Peacocks and ducks

They wander around like they own the place. Technically, they’re citizens.
